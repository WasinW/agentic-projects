# 09 — Criteria & Frameworks

> The portable part. These are tied to no project and survive any of them being cancelled.
> If everything else in this folder is thrown away, keep this file.

## 1. The three-layer test — apply before judging any project

| Layer | Lifespan | Dies when |
|---|---|---|
| **Business model** | Many eras | The scarcity it charges for stops being scarce |
| **Product** | One era | The market saturates or an incumbent ships it |
| **Strategy** | Changeable at will | The chosen buyer can't be reached |

**Most "this project is dead" conclusions are really product-level.** Ask which layer actually failed before cancelling anything.

**Worked example — the three Track B models:**

| Model | Charges for | Scarcity trend |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace | Matching supply to demand | Dead horizontally · still scarce vertically |
| Sovereignty | Control and ownership | **Increasing** |
| Accountability | Someone to bear liability | **Increasing** with agent count |

All three models are fine. All three products died. The strategy error was shared: **buyers that a solo operator cannot reach** — global developers, governments, enterprises with procurement. Each requires distribution, certifications, or a sales team.

## 2. The three portable filters

Run any idea through these. They apply to businesses, investments and career moves alike.

### Filter 1 — Where does the source data live?
- **On the internet** → red ocean. Anyone can build it. You are always late.
- **Inside machines, processes, un-digitised documents, or a language the models weren't tuned for** → blue. Access is required before anyone can build at all.

> Content is red because its data is entirely on the net — not because it is content.

### Filter 2 — Can the buyer be reached without anyone's permission?
If reaching them needs procurement, certification, a local entity, references or a sales team, a solo operator cannot get there regardless of product quality.

**Reachable:** yourself · your own audience · individuals · small local firms · audit practices
**Not reachable solo:** enterprises with procurement · governments · global developer populations

### Filter 3 — Is the scarcity being charged for increasing or decreasing?
A model whose scarcity is eroding dies even if the product is excellent. A model whose scarcity is growing survives product death.

### The one-line version
> Don't ask whether you're selling an agent or a solution. Ask **who can reach the data that makes it work.** "Everyone" means don't build it. "Only people inside" means it's yours.

## 3. Supporting tests (from earlier in the sessions)

**The four-filter viability test** *(brought by Sin from a parallel conversation)*
1. Is my edge **access** or **code**? Code has ~6 months of life; access lasts years.
2. Does the buyer already have this budget? Creating a new budget line doubles the difficulty.
3. Can I get a first paying customer without anyone's permission?
4. If a hyperscaler ships this next month, am I dead?

> Use as a warning system, not a verdict machine — applied strictly it would have killed Stripe at founding. But when a specific incumbent has *already shipped*, that is fact, not forecast.

**The change filter**
> If the world changed twice as fast, would this be worth more or less?

Build things that *benefit* from change — intermediaries, converters, registries, standards, migration tooling, verification. Every new thing that appears gives them more work.

**The capability test**
> If this works, who can do what they couldn't yesterday?

Replaces "is this new?" Innovation lives in newly-possible human capability, not business-model novelty. Almost no large company won by being first.

**The byproduct pattern** *(Sin's own formulation)*
> Structure the commercial business so the visionary goal is produced as a *byproduct*, not funded separately afterwards.

Stronger than "make money first, then dream," because the dream sits in the cost structure from day one and can't be quietly dropped.

## 4. What is blue ocean now — shortlist

Ordered by reachability for a solo operator with Thai/SEA access and a data-engineering background.

| # | Direction | Why it's blue |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agents fed by live operational data** — machine telemetry, CDC from back-office systems, production-line logs | The market is full of agent builders and nearly empty of people who can make enterprise data agent-ready. Data isn't on the internet. |
| 2 | **Agents over Thai documents and Thai rules** — permits, zoning, BOI, financials, government notices | Too small a market and too linguistically hard for the majors. Requires reading Thai. |
| 3 | **Agents for lagging sectors** — healthcare, government | Banking/insurance already ~47% deployed; the laggards are unclaimed but need access |
| 4 | **The dataset of how agents fail** | Not software. Only produced by running real fleets. Sellable later to insurers and auditors. |

**Explicitly red — do not enter:** content · customer service · research/summarisation (~25% of the agent market) · observability · horizontal governance.

**Market context:** ready-to-deploy horizontal agents hold >85% share; vertical build-your-own agents carry the higher growth rate; Asia Pacific is the fastest-growing region.

## 5. What gets sold in the ASI era

Five things. **None of them is software.**

1. **Rights of access to non-copyable things** — energy, land, licences, grid interconnection queues
2. **Bearing liability** — someone or some legal entity must sign; AI cannot structurally
3. **Proof of humanity** — when everything can be synthesised
4. **Taste** — deciding what *should* be made, once anything *can* be made
5. **Data that only exists from having acted** — uncopyable by definition

All five are positions or rights, not products.

## 6. What can be owned that passes all three of Sin's own criteria

*AI can't do it · grows while you sleep · gets more expensive as AI gets better*

| Asset | Why it passes |
|---|---|
| **An audience that trusts you** | Content floods and cheapens; trust doesn't. Grows while you sleep. |
| **A dataset that exists nowhere on the internet** | `post_log` tagged C×T×M with real outcomes across verticals, in Thai. Unscrapeable — it only exists because someone acted. Better models extract more value from the same data. |
| **Legal or professional standing** | Licences, certifications, signature rights. AI can't hold them structurally, not just technically. |
| **Being the reference point of a category** | AI can copy it but not replace it; more systems mean more need for shared vocabulary. This is the content-led-authority route. |
| **A place, not content** | Owning where people gather rather than what's posted there. Survives you stopping production. |
| **Rights to scarce physical things** | Land, water, energy access. Buildable, but needs starting capital. |

**Does NOT pass:** skills (don't grow while you sleep; cheapen as AI improves) · software with no users (fails all three) · **content itself** (cheapens — what appreciates is the *relationship* content creates, not the content).

## 7. The flexible structure

```
┌─ DUPLICATE AND REUSE — replaced each era ────────┐
│  PRODUCT — agents, tools, features                │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
┌─ NEVER DISCARD ───────────────────────────────────┐
│  ① The buyer relationship / audience              │
│  ② Data accumulated from real operation           │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

When the era turns, replace only the top layer. The two below carry across.

This is why Amazon moved from books → everything → cloud → advertising on the same customers and the same behavioural data. **The product is not made flexible; it is made replaceable, while the two layers beneath it are made permanent.**

## 8. Layer birth triggers

**General rule: a layer is born when *not* having it costs more than building it.** Never before. Any of them may never be born — that is an acceptable outcome, not a failure.

| Layer | Observable trigger | First version |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketplace** | Someone outside asks — unprompted — to sell to your audience; or your audience asks for something you don't make | A spreadsheet and a link. Broker one deal by hand. Build only after you become the bottleneck across several. |
| **Accountability** | An agent does something wrong and you can't identify which one; or an outsider asks how something was made | PreToolUse hook + hash-chained log. ~4 hrs/month, already permitted by Regent ADR-0001. |
| **Revenue-share** | Money flowing to more than two parties, where splitting by hand costs real time or causes disputes | A database and a rule. |
| **Blockchain specifically** | **A counterparty asks how they can be sure you won't change the split** | Its function is credible commitment where trust is absent — not efficiency. Until asked, a database wins on every dimension. |

**Only one requires action today.** Raw material differs by whether it can be collected retroactively:
- Audience — already accumulating via Lumora
- Transaction records — arrive with the marketplace
- **Logs — cannot be backfilled. Uncaptured logs are lost permanently.**

**So: log correctly from post #1. That is the entire present-day obligation of the deferred layers.**

## 9. Disciplines to hold (Sin's own, worth keeping)

- **A list of skills never scaffolded is fake progress, not an asset.** Applies equally to named projects with no artifact.
- **A parked project must carry zero recurring maintenance** — no re-research, no refresh. Otherwise it isn't parked.
- **A dead thing stated clearly is cheaper than a dead thing left ambiguous.** Soft-parking keeps re-inviting build sessions.
- **Don't rename parked projects.** Renaming is motion without information gain, makes dead things feel alive, and destroys the record of why they died. Refer to unborn layers by function; name them when a trigger fires.
- **Don't validate more than one catalog at once.**
- **Wait for the second use case** before extracting or abstracting anything.

## 10. Method errors recorded so they aren't repeated

1. **TAM without competitive structure.** Citing market size and growth without checking who already occupies the position.
2. **Specifying architecture without reading the project's canonical docs.**
3. **Not checking for a binding ADR** before proposing a build.
4. **Sourcing from content marketing** — trading-platform blogs, "top picks" listicles — while warning against exactly those sources.
5. **Judging at product level and calling it a verdict on the project.**
6. **Answering "focus" when the question was "explore."**
