## PART 2 — HOW TO WORK THROUGH THIS

**Structural note before the list:** across all 14 tiers, ask which layer captures value *regardless of which AI company wins*. Historically in infrastructure booms, demand materialises as forecast while the first wave of investors still loses money — because supply overbuilds and prices compete away. Railways and 2000-era fibre both followed that shape. The layers that survived were the ones with a physical constraint that could not be built around quickly: rights of way, interconnection queues, licensed fuel, multi-year-lead-time equipment.

**Suggested screening order per name** — the questions that separate a real position from a story:
1. Is it still listed and independent? *(9 of the source list's 100 failed here)*
2. What share of revenue is actually AI/data-center exposed? Many industrials have <10%.
3. Is it a component supplier, or does it own a constrained asset? Constrained assets — grid interconnection, licensed fuel, land, long-lead manufacturing — hold up better through an overbuild.
4. Who is the customer? Selling to four hyperscalers is concentration risk, not a moat.
5. Is the AI angle in the revenue or only in the press release?
6. Does the price already discount 2030?
